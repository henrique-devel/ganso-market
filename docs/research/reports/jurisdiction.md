# jurisdiction

## Summary
Polymarket's compliance landscape changed drastically in 2025-2026, and Brazil moved from unregulated gray zone to explicit prohibition. As of the official Polymarket Help Center page (last updated 2026-08-14), Brazil is on the blocked-jurisdictions list (country code BR), alongside ~39 fully blocked countries including the US (main offshore exchange), UK, France, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Belgium, Poland, Australia, Japan, Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand and OFAC-sanctioned states; some countries (Singapore, Poland, Thailand, Taiwan — and per third-party trackers also Brazil) are 'close-only': existing positions can be closed and funds withdrawn, but no new trades. The trigger for Brazil was CMN Resolution 5.298/2026 (adopted 2026-04-24, in force 2026-05-04), which prohibits offering/trading derivatives whose underlying is sports, virtual gaming, political, electoral, social, cultural or entertainment events not representing 'economic-financial benchmarks'; in parallel, the SPA (Secretaria de Prêmios e Apostas / Ministério da Fazenda, Technical Note SEI 2958/2026/MF) concluded prediction markets reproduce fixed-odds betting under Lei 14.790/2023 and are therefore illegal unlicensed lottery operations (a license costs R$30M and requires a Brazilian entity), and Anatel blocked 27-28 platforms including Polymarket and Kalshi. TSE Resolution 23.735/2024 separately treats election-outcome betting as an electoral-law violation. Official enforcement statements target operators, not individual users, but user-level exposure (e.g., gambling misdemeanor under LCP art. 50) is unresolved and must be checked with Brazilian counsel.

In the US, Polymarket returned via the $112M acquisition of CFTC-licensed QCEX (July 2025), creating QCX LLC d/b/a 'Polymarket US', a Designated Contract Market (CFTC no-action/approval Sept 3, 2025; Amended Order of Designation Nov 2025; invite-only app rollout from Dec 3, 2025; waitlist removed May 2026). Polymarket US requires full KYC (government ID, SSN, US residency proof) — US access is therefore only via the regulated KYC'd entity, while the international platform still blocks the US. The international platform (operated by Adventure One QSS Inc., Panama; disputes resolved in Panama) still generally requires no KYC for standard email/wallet signup, though high-volume API/co-location traders complete separate KYC/business verification, and a 2026 'Polymarket Perps' beta introduced KYC for that product only. ToS Section 2.1 requires self-certification of not being in a Prohibited Locality, and Section 2.1.4 makes using VPNs or similar tools to bypass geographic restrictions a standalone ToS violation; detection reportedly includes VPN IP-range flagging, WebRTC leaks, browser fingerprinting and on-chain wallet history, and consequences include immediate suspension, close-only mode, termination and funds frozen for weeks-to-months with no defined appeal. Bots/automated trading via the public CLOB API are explicitly permitted (wash trading and spoofing are the prohibited behaviors) — so the bot itself is not the compliance problem; the operator's location/eligibility is.

For legitimately operating from outside Brazil: the factual requirements that emerge are (a) actually being resident/established in a jurisdiction Polymarket serves (it claims 100+ supported countries; examples cited by trackers include Argentina, Mexico, India, South Korea, Spain, Switzerland — verify each against the official list, which changes frequently and now covers most of Western Europe as blocked), (b) passing any KYC under real identity, and (c) understanding that Brazilian tax residency does not end by moving assets abroad: a Brazilian tax resident owes tax on worldwide income, and non-residency only takes effect via the formal Comunicação/Declaração de Saída Definitiva (or after 12 consecutive months of absence if not formalized). Under Lei 14.754/2023 + IN RFB 2.180/2024, income/gains from 'financial investments abroad' — which RFB interprets to include crypto custodied or traded through foreign institutions — are taxed at a flat 15% in the annual return (loss offsetting allowed, no R$35k exemption); self-custodied crypto follows the holder's tax domicile and stays under the domestic regime (monthly capital-gains rules with the R$35k/month total-disposals exemption, 15-22.5% progressive rates) — MP 1.303/2025, which would have imposed a flat 17.5% and killed the exemption, lapsed in Congress in October 2025, so the old rules stand for 2026. Reporting: IN RFB 1.888/2019 requires individuals to report crypto operations done outside Brazilian exchanges (including DEX/self-custody) above a monthly threshold; it is replaced by 'DeCripto' (IN RFB 2.291/2025, aligned to OECD CARF) with the old framework running through 2026-06-30 and DeCripto reporting starting July 2026, individual threshold raised from R$30,000 to R$35,000/month. Solana DEX trading itself (Pump.fun/PumpSwap) is not prohibited for Brazilian individuals — Brazil's crypto framework (Lei 14.478/2022, Decree 11.563/2023, and BCB Resolutions 519/520/521 of 2025-11-10, in force from 2026-02-02 with authorization deadline 2026-10-30) regulates service providers (SPSAVs/VASPs), not individuals trading their own capital; but note Resolution 521 pulls self-hosted-wallet transfers and stablecoin flows into the FX regime via identification requirements on providers, and Pump.fun itself is unlicensed everywhere, geoblocks the UK (since Dec 2024, after an FCA warning) plus sanctioned countries (Brazil is not on its blocked list), and faces a consolidated unregistered-securities/RICO class action (Aguilar v. Baton Corporation, SDNY).

## Key findings
- Polymarket officially lists Brazil as a blocked jurisdiction on its Help Center geographic-restrictions page (updated 2026-08-14); third-party trackers report Brazil is in close-only mode (withdraw/close existing positions only, no new trades) since late April 2026.
- CMN Resolution 5.298/2026 (adopted 2026-04-24, effective 2026-05-04) prohibits offering and trading in Brazil of derivatives whose underlying assets are sports events, virtual online games, or political/electoral/social/cultural/entertainment events that are not 'economic-financial benchmarks'; contracts on price indexes, interest rates, FX, commodities, securities remain possible subject to CVM discretion.
- SPA/Ministerio da Fazenda (Technical Note SEI 2958/2026/MF, position consolidated 2026-04-24) classified prediction markets as fixed-odds betting under Lei 14.790/2023, meaning offering them to Brazilian users without a license (R$30M grant + Brazilian HQ + AML/responsible-gaming rules) is illegal lottery exploitation; Anatel blocked 27-28 platforms including Polymarket and Kalshi.
- TSE Resolution 23.735/2024 prohibits betting linked to election results in Brazil (treated as electoral-crime territory per SPA statements); official enforcement statements name operators, not individual users, but individual-user exposure (e.g., LCP art. 50 gambling misdemeanor) is unaddressed in official materials — flag for Brazilian counsel.
- Polymarket US timeline: $112M QCEX acquisition announced July 2025 (PRNewswire); CFTC go-ahead for QCX exchange 2025-09-03 (CoinDesk); Amended Order of Designation Nov 2025; invite-only US app rollout began 2025-12-03; waitlist removed May 2026; April 2026 CFTC filing seeks approval to let US users trade directly on the offshore exchange; a CFTC probe into Polymarket's marketing practices (paid creators staging fake winning bets, ~140M views) opened around June 2026.
- Polymarket US requires full KYC: government ID, SSN, proof of US residency. The international platform (Adventure One QSS Inc., Panama; Panama dispute resolution) still allows email/wallet signup without KYC for standard use, but high-volume API/co-location traders undergo separate KYC + business verification; the 2026 'Polymarket Perps' beta requires KYC for that product only (Polymarket publicly clarified core platform unchanged).
- Polymarket ToS Section 2.1 requires users to self-certify they are not residing in/located in a Prohibited Locality; Section 2.1.4 makes VPN/location-spoofing a standalone violation; reported detection: VPN IP-range lists, WebRTC leaks, browser fingerprint (timezone/language) checks, and on-chain wallet history review; consequences documented: immediate suspension, close-only mode, permanent termination, funds frozen weeks-to-months, no defined appeals process (TechRadar reported a VPN/verification crackdown in May 2026 as 30+ countries restrict the platform).
- Automated trading/bots via Polymarket's CLOB API are explicitly permitted under the ToS; prohibited behaviors are market manipulation such as wash trading and spoofing — the bot is fine, the operator's jurisdiction/eligibility is the compliance issue.
- Enforcement precedent against Polymarket entities: Jan 2022 CFTC settlement ($1.4M, US users blocked since); Nov 2024 FBI raid on CEO (DOJ/CFTC probes dropped July 2025); Ontario Securities Commission settlement fined Blockratize/Adventure One CAD ~$243k (March 2025) for offering binary options to Ontario residents; Nevada TRO and Tennessee cease-and-desist against Polymarket US (Jan 2026); Netherlands KSA halt order (Feb 2026); Portugal 48-hour shutdown order (Jan 2026).
- Brazilian tax residency: residents are taxed on worldwide income; non-residency requires the Comunicação de Saída Definitiva (by Feb 28 of the following year) plus the Declaração de Saída Definitiva — otherwise non-resident status only begins after 12 consecutive months of absence; after formalized exit, only Brazilian-source income is taxed (via withholding).
- Lei 14.754/2023 + IN RFB 2.180/2024 (published 2024-03-13): flat 15% annual IRPF on income/gains from financial investments abroad, with gain/loss offsetting, settled in the annual return; RFB's stated interpretation: crypto custodied or traded through an institution located abroad = 'financial investment abroad' (15% regime, no R$35k exemption), while self-custodied crypto follows the holder's fiscal domicile and stays under domestic rules unless later custodied/traded via a foreign institution.
- Domestic crypto capital-gains rules survive into 2026 unchanged: MP 1.303/2025 (flat 17.5% on all crypto gains, ending the exemption) lapsed/was withdrawn in Congress in October 2025 (251x193 vote to pull it), so the R$35k/month total-disposals exemption and 15-22.5% progressive rates still apply.
- Crypto transaction reporting: IN RFB 1.888/2019 obliges Brazilian-resident individuals to file monthly reports when moving > R$30k/month in crypto outside Brazilian exchanges (foreign exchanges, DEXs, self-custody transfers); replaced by DeCripto (IN RFB 2.291/2025, implementing OECD CARF): old framework valid until 2026-06-30, DeCripto filings start July 2026, individual monthly threshold raised to R$35,000.
- Solana DEX trading by an individual with own capital is not a regulated/prohibited activity in Brazil: Lei 14.478/2022 + Decree 11.563/2023 + BCB Resolutions 519/520/521 (published 2025-11-10; force 2026-02-02; existing-provider authorization deadline 2026-10-30; full Travel Rule 2028-02-02) regulate service providers (SPSAVs) only; Resolution 521 subjects transfers to/from self-hosted wallets and stablecoin flows to FX-regime identification rules at the provider level.
- Pump.fun: unlicensed in every jurisdiction, geoblocked the UK in Dec 2024 after an FCA unauthorized-firm warning, also blocks Russia, Iran, Syria, Cuba, North Korea; Brazil is NOT on its blocked list; consolidated class action Aguilar v. Baton Corporation (SDNY) alleges unregistered securities sales with RICO claims also naming Solana Labs and Solana Foundation (motion to dismiss pending as of research date).
- Conflicting data flag: third-party trackers (e.g., Datawallet) list New Zealand and others as 'supported' while Polymarket's own Help Center lists New Zealand as blocked — the official Help Center list changes frequently (updated the day before this research) and must be treated as the only authoritative source, re-checked at build time.

## Pitfalls
- Brazil is now a hard-blocked jurisdiction for Polymarket (both by Polymarket policy and by Brazilian regulation since April/May 2026). Any plan to trade Polymarket while physically located in or tax-resident-and-present in Brazil, or to 'appear' to be elsewhere, is a ToS Section 2.1/2.1.4 violation with documented consequences: account suspension without notice, close-only lockdown, permanent termination, and funds frozen for weeks-to-months with no defined appeal. Legitimate operation requires genuine physical presence/residence in an allowed jurisdiction — this is a fact to establish with immigration/tax professionals, not a technical configuration.
- Ambiguity risk: 'operating from abroad' with a server/VPS while the human operator remains in Brazil is untested territory — the ToS eligibility clause looks at where the user resides/is located, not where the bot runs; do not assume a non-Brazil VPS satisfies eligibility. Get a professional opinion before committing capital.
- Polymarket's blocked list is volatile (France early 2025 → UK, Netherlands, Portugal, Brazil, more through 2026; 30+ countries now restricted, EU-wide pressure ongoing). A jurisdiction chosen today can flip to close-only with little notice — build for orderly position wind-down and fund withdrawal, and re-check the official Help Center list at every milestone rather than relying on cached third-party lists (they already conflict, e.g., New Zealand).
- Election-linked markets carry extra Brazilian exposure: TSE Resolution 23.735/2024 treats election-outcome betting as electoral-crime territory — a category to exclude entirely for anyone with Brazilian ties, regardless of platform policy.
- Brazilian official enforcement so far targets operators (Anatel blocking, R$30M licensing), and the government stated no retroactive criminalization of users — but individual-user liability under LCP art. 50 (gambling misdemeanor) has not been officially clarified; treat as an open legal question for counsel, not a cleared risk.
- Tax residency does not follow the server or the wallet: a Brazilian tax resident owes Brazilian tax on worldwide trading profits until the Saída Definitiva is formally completed (or 12 months of absence elapse). Skipping the Comunicação/Declaração de Saída while living abroad leaves worldwide taxation (and the Lei 14.754 offshore regime) fully applicable.
- Two different Brazilian crypto tax regimes can apply to the same strategy: assets custodied/traded via foreign platforms (e.g., USDC balances on Polymarket) fall under the Lei 14.754/IN 2180 flat-15% annual regime (no R$35k exemption), while self-custodied Solana DEX trading arguably stays under the domestic monthly regime (R$35k/month exemption, 15-22.5%) — but whether DEX trades from self-custody count as 'traded through a foreign institution' is an unsettled interpretive question; a wrong classification means wrong tax and penalties. Professional sign-off needed.
- Reporting obligations bite even when no tax is due: IN 1888 (until 2026-06-30) then DeCripto/IN 2291 (from July 2026) require monthly reports for >R$30k (rising to R$35k) of crypto operations outside Brazilian exchanges — a high-frequency Pump.fun bot will cross this threshold almost immediately, and each month's report is due by the last business day of the following month.
- Do not conflate Polymarket US with Polymarket international: the US entity (QCX) requires SSN-based KYC and is only for US residents; the international platform blocks the US; an April 2026 CFTC filing to merge access is pending, and a June 2026 CFTC probe plus state-level actions (Nevada TRO, Tennessee C&D) mean the US regime is still in flux.
- Platform risk on the Solana leg: Pump.fun is unlicensed in every jurisdiction, already geoblocked one G7 country (UK) on regulator demand, and faces a consolidated securities/RICO class action — its ToS, fee structure, or availability can change abruptly; the bot design should not hard-depend on Pump.fun remaining available or unchanged.
- MP 1.303's lapse is not permanent safety: the government can reintroduce the 17.5% flat crypto tax as an ordinary bill; the R$35k exemption's survival should be re-verified each tax year.
- KYC 'not required' on Polymarket international is conditional: high-volume API/co-location traders face KYC + business verification, and Polymarket has been tightening verification since May 2026 — a profitable bot is exactly the profile that gets escalated, and KYC under a real identity showing a blocked-country residence would end the account.
