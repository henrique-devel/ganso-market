# gap:Real-time information feeds that power probability estimation — access, cost, latency, and licensing for individual operators

## Summary
Feed access in late 2026 splits cleanly into a solo-viable tier and an enterprise-gated tier, and the split maps directly onto which documented Polymarket strategies remain reproducible. SPORTS: The Odds API (the-odds-api.com) is the only realistic sharp-odds source for an individual — free 500 credits/mo, then $30/20K, $59/100K, $119/5M, $249/15M credits per month, with Pinnacle (EU region, sourced from the public site "may incur a delay"), Betfair Exchange, Matchbook, and even a US-exchanges region that includes Polymarket/Kalshi prices. Update cadence is 60s pre-match / 40s in-play for featured markets and 20s/10s for exchange odds; its terms do not prohibit betting use (only raw-data redistribution). Sportradar/Stats Perform are effectively enterprise-only (~$5–10k/mo starter contracts, annual commitment, sales-gated), as are OpticOdds and OddsJam's API ($499–$5,000+/mo quotes; OddsJam's consumer scanner is $199–$999/mo with no API). Betfair's own Exchange API is individually accessible but requires a KYC'd, funded Betfair account in a permitted jurisdiction (UK/IE/MT full access; IT/ES/AU via local licences; US/CA/FR/NL and most of Asia blocked; no Brazil exchange documented) plus a one-off £499 live-key fee — and read-only live keys are not permitted. Given Polymarket's 2026 sports frictions (3-second delay on marketable orders, 1-second taker-delay tests on NBA/MLB, taker fees at a 0.05 sports rate maxing ~$1.25/100 shares, makers $0 with 15% of taker fees rebated to makers), the surviving sports play is exactly what kachence/polymm did: de-vig Pinnacle via The Odds API and quote maker-side. That code is public and mechanically reproducible for $30–59/mo, but its author retired it after ~$5k net because faster competitors and adverse selection on unhedged legs ate the edge — treat pre-game/maker de-vig as reproducible, in-play odds-lag taking as dead (40s feed refresh sits far inside the delay window).

ESPORTS is the category that flipped from solo-accessible to gated. The documented $208k-in-3-months TeemuTeemuTeemu bot monetized the 15–60s+ gap between game-server data and Twitch/broadcast delay on LoL/Dota in-play markets. In 2026 the cheap paths to that data are closing: Riot's Live Client Data API is local-client only with a 3-minute spectator delay, official LoL/VALORANT esports data is distributed exclusively through GRID (riotesportsdata.com, sales-gated), GRID Open Access is free for independent developers but covers CS2/Dota community feeds while the betting-grade live event product ("Series Events") is paid/commercial, and PandaScore's self-serve plans are explicitly "only available to customers with non betting-related usage" — its €1,000/mo/game Live Basic feed is additionally stream-synced (deliberately no ahead-of-stream edge), with the fast feed reserved for negotiated betting contracts. One prominent counterclaim (Daniel Luca on X) notes tournament realms run ≥5-minute delays, so the residual edge is parsing speed vs other watchers, not raw server access. Verdict: the esports latency edge is now enterprise-gated or ToS-prohibited for individuals; only scraping community endpoints (bo3.gg wrappers, unofficial lolesports feeds) remains, at legal/ban risk and unquantified latency.

WEATHER and CRYPTO remain fully open. Open-Meteo's free tier (10,000 calls/day, 600/min, no key) includes the ensemble endpoints the documented suislanchez Kalshi-KXHIGH/Polymarket temperature bot uses (31-member GFS ensemble + free NWS observations; "100% free to run"), though the free licence is non-commercial — a for-profit bot should budget the commercial API (Standard 1M/Professional 5M calls/mo; ensemble commercially licensed at Professional+; exact prices not published on-page, historically ~€29–99/mo, uncertain). ECMWF made its entire real-time catalogue open: 0.25° IFS/AIFS forecasts free under CC-BY-4.0 (commercial use explicitly allowed) via data.ecmwf.int/AWS/GCS, with 9 km data at ~2h latency promised later in 2026 — this is the single best free upgrade for temperature-bucket probability models. NWS api.weather.gov is free US-government open data (unpublished rate limit, ~5k/hr anecdotal, User-Agent header required) and is the settlement source for Kalshi KXHIGH. Binance WebSocket market streams remain free without authentication (1024 streams/connection, 300 connections per 5 min per IP), fully adequate for Polymarket 5–60-min up/down markets — the real work is reconstructing Polymarket's "price to beat," which has no API. NEWS/X: the official X API moved to pay-per-use in Feb 2026 with no free tier ($0.005/post read ⇒ $5/1k tweets; legacy $200 Basic being force-migrated since June 2026), making official access impractical for high-volume solo monitoring; operators use third-party scraper APIs (twitterapi.io ~$0.15/1k tweets, GetXAPI ~$0.05/1k — ToS-grey). NewsAPI.org is $449/mo for commercial real-time; GNews from ~$84/mo (free tier is 12h-delayed). Research on Polymarket news repricing reports fastest wallets act in seconds but full repricing takes ~1–30 min (one study: ~80 min to complete), so a $84–150/mo news stack plus LLM classification targets the minutes-scale window, not the seconds war. For Solana memecoin social signals, LunarCrush's API ($72–$720/mo, uncertain) and DIY Telegram monitoring via user-session clients (free) are the norm; little beyond that is worth budgeting versus the on-chain feeds already covered.

## Pitfalls
- In-play sports latency-taking on Polymarket is structurally dead for feed-based solo bots: The Odds API refreshes in-play featured markets every 40s while Polymarket imposes a 3s marketable-order delay, 1s taker-delay tests, and 0.05-rate taker fees — any odds-lag signal is stale and taxed before it executes; only pre-game de-vig and maker-side quoting (fees $0 + 15% rebate share) remain rational
- PandaScore's self-serve plans explicitly exclude betting-related usage — running a Polymarket bot on a €400–1,000/mo self-serve plan is a ToS violation risking cutoff; its Live Basic feed is also deliberately stream-synced, so even paying does not buy an ahead-of-stream edge
- GRID Open Access is free for individuals but does NOT include the betting-grade Series Events feed; assuming the free CS2/Dota feed reproduces the TeemuTeemuTeemu edge is unverified — the fast official feeds are enterprise/betting-contract gated, and Riot spectator data carries a 3-minute delay
- Sportradar, Stats Perform/Opta, OpticOdds, and OddsJam API are enterprise-only in practice (roughly $5k–10k+/mo, annual commitments, sales-gated) — any roadmap line item assuming individual access to official low-latency sports data is wrong
- Betfair API requires an exchange account in a permitted jurisdiction (no US/CA/FR/NL/most of Asia; Brazil exchange not documented as available), a £499 non-refundable live-key fee, and prohibits read-only live keys — using it purely as a de-vig data source without betting through it is not a permitted configuration; the free delayed key's data lag makes it unsuitable for fast repricing
- Open-Meteo's free tier is non-commercial only — a profit-generating trading bot is arguably commercial use, so budget the paid licence (ensemble requires Professional tier or higher for commercial use); exact 2026 plan prices were not verifiable on-page (historic ~€29–99/mo, uncertain)
- Official X API has no free tier since Feb 2026 and costs $5 per 1,000 tweet reads — naive firehose-style monitoring costs thousands/month; third-party scraper APIs (twitterapi.io etc.) are 30–100x cheaper but violate X's ToS and can vanish, so treat them as replaceable plumbing, not a dependency
- GNews' free tier delays articles 12 hours and NewsAPI's free tier is non-commercial — free news tiers are useless for event trading; the real commercial floor is ~$84/mo (GNews) to $449/mo (NewsAPI Business)
- SofaScore/FlashScore have no official API; Apify-style scrapers (5–15s claimed latency) operate against the sites' terms — account/IP bans and silent latency degradation are operational risks, and their official ToS were not directly verified in this research
- Speed claims in vendor/SEO content (e.g. '73% of arb profits to sub-100ms bots', '80-minute full repricing') come from low-rigor sources — use them as directional, not planning, numbers; similarly the 20K-tier pricing appeared as both $29 and $30 across sources (the-odds-api.com's own page said $30)
- Polymarket crypto up/down markets: the data problem is not the exchange feed (Binance WS is free and sufficient) but Polymarket itself — no API exposes the 'price to beat', and the CLOB sits in AWS eu-west-2, so a solo bot without London-region co-location loses fills to faster wallets regardless of feed quality
- Enterprise data licensing (Sportradar, GRID betting tier, PandaScore betting contracts) typically requires being a licensed betting operator or B2B entity — a solo individual trading personal capital generally cannot even sign these contracts, independent of price
